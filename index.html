The field at a glance

Being competent about AI models means you can read a launch announcement, a paper or a price sheet and know what is being claimed, what it costs and what could go wrong. That takes working literacy in about forty subfields. The map lays them out the way the field is organized: a model's life cycle from data to deployed agent, the architecture at its center, three lenses that study it (evaluation, interpretability, safety), the math and research practice underneath, and the economics and policy around it.

Open any subfield for a one-page guide: what it is, the key ideas, why it matters, an investor lens, what competence looks like, a self-check, the canonical reading and a hands-on exercise. Mark your level as you go; it is saved in this browser.

Three ways to do AI research

    The one chart that explains modern LLM economics

    log-log · hover any point

    Total parameters set the memory bill (the whole model must sit in GPU RAM). Active parameters set the compute bill (what actually runs per token). Dense models live on the diagonal — cost rises with size. Mixture-of-experts models fall far below it: huge capacity, small per-token cost. The frontier's entire post-2024 story is the widening gap between these two numbers.

    The march toward sparsity

    share of parameters that fire per token, by release date
    Dense (100% active) Mixture of Experts
    Investor takeaway: Mixtral used 28% of its weights per token (Dec '23). DeepSeek-V3: 5.5%. gpt-oss and Qwen3-Next: ~4%. Every step down this curve is a structural cut to the compute cost of intelligence — deflationary for inference pricing, and the reason "token demand" and "GPU demand" are no longer the same trade.
